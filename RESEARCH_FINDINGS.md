# Research findings

The corrected simulation-version-2 experiments compare existing allocation policies on synthetic borrowers and repayment outcomes. The contribution is a reproducible progression from group-level bandits to applicant-level learning and delayed portfolio cash accounting, with auditable fairness denominators, policy comparisons and negative results.

The phases and sweeps in a given configuration reuse its seed list.

Reward counts successful repayments. Repayment rate divides those successes by loans funded, and reward per cost divides them by principal disbursed. Ratios are computed per seed and then averaged. Phase 3 cumulative reward counts eventual successes on funded loans. Observed successes use the maturity cutoff at the advance into the decision horizon. See the [metric equations](MATHEMATICAL_FORMULATION.md#primary-evaluation-metrics).

Continuous summaries use sample standard deviation and 95% Student’s t intervals with (n-1) degrees of freedom. Episode pass proportions use 95% Wilson intervals. Paired differences are computed on matched seeds within a configuration, with valid-pair counts and t intervals in `paired_comparisons.csv`. Missing outcomes and evaluability are tracked separately. Undefined parity contributes no joint pass to the all-episode denominator. The comparisons below that have no saved paired interval are descriptive. Configuration and sweep counts describe tested settings, which can share seeds and baseline cases. They supply no pooled estimate or inference across phases or settings.

## Phase 1 group-level bandits

Budgeted UCB (`budgeted_ucb`) averaged 67.4 successes at a 53.18% episode-mean repayment rate against UCB's (`ucb`) 45.1 successes at 67.72%. That is 22.3 more successes, or 49.45% relative to 45.1. The mean allocation-share gap rose from 0.3346 to 0.7570. These directions held in all 11 configurations for this pair. A higher success rate can coincide with fewer total successes when expensive loans reduce the number that can be funded. Within an episode the success count is funded volume times the realised repayment rate. The study averages each quantity separately.

Budgeted UCB and Fractional KUBE (`fractional_kube`) use the same index and selection under matched states and parameters. The default mean for both was 67.4. KUBE (`kube`) averaged 68.2 and the informed greedy benchmark (`true_reward_per_cost`) averaged 73.4. The informed benchmark led by reward in eight of 11 configurations. KUBE led in scenario 2, and soft-fair Budgeted UCB led in scenarios 8 and 9.

Against its unwrapped base, hard-fair Budgeted UCB (`fair_ucb_hard`) reduced the default allocation-share gap by 47.11 percentage points on average (paired 95% interval −49.93 to −44.30) and produced 15.6 fewer successes (−18.80 to −12.40). It passed both terminal thresholds in 10 of 10 default episodes while meeting, on average, 83.33% of individual quota targets within the numerical quota tolerance. The joint pass uses the allowed **total** deficit of 0.01. The hard variant passed every seed in eight configurations and missed at least one in scenarios 5, 6 and 7. Compared with `fair_ucb_soft`, it had a lower mean gap, a higher fraction of individual quotas met and fewer successes in all 11 configurations.

## Phase 2 contextual bandits

Each budgeted variant (`budgeted_linucb`, `budgeted_contextual_epsilon_greedy`, `budgeted_contextual_thompson`) earned more mean successes than its corresponding cost-unaware variant in every configuration. In the default run, budgeted LinUCB averaged 112.0 successes and funded 150 loans per seed, compared with 41.5 successes and 52.6 loans for `linucb`. LinUCB used 99.92% of initial budget on average and budgeted LinUCB used 44.21%. The mean feasible-opportunity gap fell from 7.42 to 3.34 percentage points while the population gap rose from 2.53 to 3.34 points. Affordability and funding volume affect which applicants enter the feasible denominator. This is a comparison of cost scoring among contextual policies.

Hard-fair LinUCB (`fair_linucb_hard`) led the default feasible-joint ranking with 6 of 10 passes. Its mean gap was 1.19 percentage points at a 1.00-point threshold. The paired mean feasible gap difference against `budgeted_linucb` was −2.15 points (95% interval −3.16 to −1.15). The mean reward difference was +0.4 successes (−3.95 to +4.75), accompanied by lower reward per cost and higher budget use. All three hard variants had smaller mean feasible gaps and lower reward per cost than their corresponding soft variants across all 11 configurations. Quota outcomes often tied. Joint compliance was higher for the hard variants except for an epsilon-greedy tie at zero in scenario 4. There was no all-seed joint pass by any policy in any of these 11 Phase 2 runs.

The default reward leader was budgeted contextual epsilon-greedy (114.7) followed by the informed greedy benchmark (`true_contextual_reward_per_cost`, 114.4). The benchmark led outright in seven configurations and tied for first with budgeted contextual Thompson sampling in scenario 5. Hard-fair Thompson sampling led in scenario 2 and soft-fair Thompson sampling in scenario 7.

## Phase 3 portfolio policies

Hard-fair portfolio LinUCB (`cmdp_fair_hard_linucb`) led the default feasible-joint ranking with 9 of 10 passes. The informed portfolio heuristic (`oracle_portfolio`) averaged 368.9 eventual successes, 352.4 observed successes and 3 of 10 joint passes. Its default score subtracts a liquidity-dependent normalized cost penalty from the true repayment probability. It led by eventual successes in ten configurations. The context-only cost-normalized control (`context_only_budgeted_linucb`) led in scenario 10.

Hard-fair portfolio LinUCB averaged 354.6 eventual successes, 338.1 observed successes and turnover of 2.89 times initial capital. Its paired difference against `portfolio_linucb` was −0.2 eventual successes (95% interval −6.31 to +5.91), with a feasible gap change of −3.68 percentage points (−5.16 to −2.19). It had lower mean reward per principal. All three hard portfolio variants had lower mean feasible gaps and higher joint pass fractions than the corresponding adaptive Lagrangian variants in the 11 main configurations, with lower mean reward per cost in every comparison. Eventual reward could increase, as for default Thompson sampling (351.3 versus 346.7). Among these main Phase 3 runs, hard-fair LinUCB in scenario 2 was the sole all-seed joint pass.

The unwrapped portfolio LinUCB and context-only control both filled all 450 default slots per seed. Portfolio LinUCB yielded 354.8 eventual successes versus 335.5 and had turnover of 2.76 versus 1.79. Across the 11 configurations, portfolio LinUCB funded fewer loans in eight and the same mean number in three. Eventual reward was higher in five and lower in six, with lower mean reward per unit principal in all 11. Both policies operate in the same recycling environment, but cost normalization and state-dependent cost subtraction are different score rules. Their results do not identify a separate effect of portfolio information.

## Phase 3 sensitivity sweeps

- **Initial capital.** At half the default initial capital, the informed portfolio heuristic averaged 364.6 eventual successes versus 368.9 at baseline (−1.17%), with feasible parity gap rising from 0.0800 to 0.0916 (+1.17 percentage points from unrounded means). Hard-fair LinUCB passed both thresholds in 7 of 10 episodes at half capital and 9 of 10 at baseline. All three hard families met every individual quota at each tested capital level, with joint compliance varying by family and setting. [Aggregate](outputs/phase4.1/cmdp_robustness/sweeps/budget/aggregate_results.csv).
- **Group imbalance.** Hard-fair LinUCB passed both thresholds in 0, 9, 9, 10 and 7 of 10 episodes at marginalized-community generator probabilities of 10%, 20%, 30%, 40% and 50%, respectively. At 10% its mean feasible gap was 0.0783 versus 0.0517 for Lagrangian LinUCB. Both had zero joint passes at that setting. The probability controls a generator attribute and overlaps with groups that have higher primary-label priority. [Aggregate](outputs/phase4.1/cmdp_robustness/sweeps/group_imbalance/aggregate_results.csv).
- **Fairness strength.** Hard-fair LinUCB's smallest mean feasible gap was 0.0360 at strength 1.25, with 349.9 mean eventual successes versus 352.0 at strength zero (−0.60% relative to zero strength). Hard-fair Thompson sampling instead gained 1.70% in mean eventual successes at its lowest-gap strength (0.75) relative to its zero-strength run. Hard strength scales quota targets and the quota evaluation, retaining the parity override at zero. Lagrangian strength scales the penalty on scores and retains quota targets and multiplier updates. Comparisons across their strength values consequently use different quota conditions. [Aggregate](outputs/phase4.1/cmdp_robustness/sweeps/fairness_strength/aggregate_results.csv).
- **Across scenarios.** Of 165 matched family-by-setting comparisons in the capital sweeps across all 11 configurations, hard variants had higher observed joint compliance in 163, tied in one and had lower compliance in one. In the group-imbalance sweeps the counts were 128 higher, 35 ties and two lower. These are counts of family/setting comparisons from the [scenario and default sweep aggregates](outputs/phase4.1/), with matched settings and seed lists. They are descriptive, and shared seeds and baseline rows create dependencies.

For the default capital, imbalance and strength sweeps, respectively, 213 of 600, 146 of 600 and 205 of 360 episodes met both thresholds across all tested policies and settings. These totals describe the sweep grids. The quota and parity screens passed for every episode in these three sweeps, including episodes with violations. A screen concerns one constraint under observed opportunity counts and funded volume. Joint scheduling and funding feasibility remain unresolved by those screens. [Budget summary](outputs/phase4.1/cmdp_robustness/sweeps/budget/summary.json), [imbalance summary](outputs/phase4.1/cmdp_robustness/sweeps/group_imbalance/summary.json), [strength summary](outputs/phase4.1/cmdp_robustness/sweeps/fairness_strength/summary.json).

## Hypothesis assessment

| Hypothesis | Assessment | Defensible paper conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **H1 — Budget awareness.** Standard group-level bandits perform poorly under heterogeneous loan costs, while budget-aware policies improve cumulative reward but may worsen group concentration. | **Supported within the tested comparisons.** Budgeted UCB had higher reward and larger allocation-share gaps than UCB in all 11 Phase 1 settings. The informed and soft-fair reward leaders varied by setting. | Current-principal normalization improved the tested UCB policy's cumulative repayment count under heterogeneous costs and increased allocation concentration. |
| **H2 — Borrower context and cost awareness.** Borrower context improves decision quality when cost enters selection, while context alone need not prevent budget exhaustion. | **Partially supported.** All three matched Phase 2 cost comparisons favor cost-aware scoring for cumulative reward in 11 settings. Default unnormalized LinUCB used almost its full budget and funded fewer loans. There is no matched control isolating context. The population parity example also shows that fairness conclusions depend on the denominator. | Cost scoring improved cumulative repayments for the tested contextual learners. The independent effect of borrower context is inconclusive. |
| **H3 — Hard versus soft fairness.** Hard fairness improves quota and parity outcomes more reliably than soft penalties, with possible utility or capital-efficiency costs. | **Partially supported.** Phase 1 hard versus soft and the matched families in Phases 2–3 show lower gaps in all 11 main settings, but quota metrics frequently tie. Reward per principal fell. Total reward can increase, and the capital and imbalance sweeps have joint-compliance reversals. | Priority overrides improved observed parity for the tested main comparisons, often at lower repayment efficiency. Their joint compliance depends on setting and policy family. |
| **H4 — Portfolio awareness.** Under delayed feedback and capital recycling, portfolio-aware state may support more lending, while fairness feasibility remains a separate constraint. | **Inconclusive for an isolated state effect.** The simulator demonstrates capital reuse and separate fairness outcomes. The existing Phase 3 LinUCB comparison changes the score rule and shows no mean lending-volume advantage in any of the 11 configurations. | The experiment demonstrates lending with returned principal and delayed information. It leaves the independent effect of portfolio features on allocation unresolved. |

## Limits on the claims

| Claim | Status and treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Higher repayment rate can coincide with fewer cumulative successes. | **Supported.** The Phase 1 comparison and per-episode identity show the role of funded volume. |
| Fairness has a utility or capital-efficiency cost. | **Partially supported.** The reduction in reward per principal is consistent in the matched hard-versus-soft main comparisons. Total successes, capital use and fairness outcomes vary. |
| Context or portfolio information independently improves allocation. | **Not independently verifiable.** The available comparisons change other design choices and the study has no matched ablations isolating either source of information. |
| More model complexity guarantees improvement, informed comparisons provide optimal upper bounds, or benchmark differences measure theoretical regret. | **Unsupported.** The benchmarks use greedy local scores with privileged probabilities. Learners sometimes exceed their empirical reward, and no optimal policy or regret result is established for this setting. |
| Hard overrides ensure fairness, adaptive multipliers converge to feasible policies, or opportunity screens certify joint feasibility. | **Unsupported.** Observed violations, the separate count screens and the heuristic rules limit these claims. Quota targets and parity can conflict, and realized opportunities depend on past decisions. |
| The study proves new algorithmic guarantees or solves the CMDP. | **Unsupported.** It evaluates adaptations of existing bandits and heuristic constraint handling. The formal problem and the compressed policy inputs are distinct. |
| Synthetic allocation establishes real-world effectiveness or poverty alleviation. | **Unsupported.** The synthetic borrower generator and simplified delays, recycling and recoveries shape the outcomes. The measured reward is binary repayment. Profit and social impact are outside the evaluation. |

The audit groups are mutually exclusive primary labels selected by priority, so they cannot quantify disparities independently for every overlapping attribute. Removing direct protected indicators from the default predictor still permits demographic information through correlated features. Seeds are finite, comparisons are descriptive beyond the stated within-configuration intervals, and conclusions have limited external validity. The earlier dissertation results use different random streams and fairness definitions. Every numerical finding above uses the corrected archive.
